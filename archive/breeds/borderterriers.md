## Breed overview and temperament

Border Terrier temperament, personality, training, behavior, pros and cons, advice, and information, by [Michele Welton](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/about.html)

*Image hint — Border Terrier dog breed*

Inside your home, the Border Terrier is milder-mannered than most terriers. Yet in the field, hunting small animals, he is described as "hard as nails, game as they come, and driving in attack."

Some individuals are more work-oriented (not good pets) while others are more mellow (can be good pets if their other needs are met).

In general the Border Terrier is energetic and athletic. He tends to play rough and prefers vigorous exercise and interactive games.

But he should not be let off-leash, for there is no terrier more determined to explore and pursue anything that runs (except, perhaps, for the Jack Russell). Border Terriers are so inquisitive they often get themselves wedged into tight holes or crawlspaces trying to find out what's in there.

A secure yard or kennel run is essential for this breed, unless you adopt a more laid-back adult who has already proven himself to stay put and not try to escape.

Most Border Terriers who have been extensively socialized are bouncy and kissy with strangers, though there is timidity in some lines.

Unlike most terriers, the Border is usually sociable with other dogs and not given to fiery posturing. But he may or may not live peacefully with the family cat, and he is a business-like hunter of anything else.

Generally willing to please and sensitive to correction, the Border Terrier responds nicely to training that includes a lot of praise and encouragement. Motivate with food in moderation – Borders live for food and can become pudgy if over-indulged.

If you want a dog who...

- Is small, but sturdy and tough – not a delicate lapdog
- Has a natural appearance – unexaggerated and rather plain-looking
- Has a coat that's easier to groom than most other wirehaired terriers
- Doesn't shed too much
- Likes vigorous exercise and athletic activities
- Is sociable with strangers and other dogs, and not given to fiery posturing like many other terriers

A Border Terrier may be right for you.

If you don't want to deal with...

- The dynamic terrier temperament (see full description below)
- Providing enough exercise and activities to keep them busy
- Very strong instincts to chase other living creatures that run
- Stubbornness (mind of his own)
- Digging holes
- Barking

A Border Terrier may not be right for you.

Keep in mind that the inheritance of **temperament** is less predictable than the inheritance of **physical** traits such as size or shedding. Temperament and behavior are also shaped by raising and training.

- You can avoid some negative traits by choosing an ADULT dog from an [animal shelter](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/animal-shelters.html) or [rescue group](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/dog-breed-rescue.html). With an adult dog, you can easily see what you're getting, and plenty of adult Border Terriers have already proven themselves not to have negative characteristics.
- If you want a puppy, you can avoid some negative traits by choosing the [right breeder](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/dog-breeders.html) and the [right puppy](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/how-to-choose-a-puppy.html). Unfortunately, you usually can't tell whether a puppy has inherited temperament or health problems until he grows up.
- Finally, you can avoid some negative traits by [training your Border Terrier](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/training/articles/dog-respect-training.html) to respect you and by following the 11-step care program in my book, [11 Things You Must Do Right To Keep Your Dog Healthy and Happy](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/books/11T.html).

More traits and characteristics of the Border Terrier

If I was considering a Border Terrier, I would be most concerned about...

1. **The dynamic terrier temperament.** Most terrier breeds are remarkably similar. The same words are used over and over – quick to bark, quick to chase, lively, bossy, feisty, scrappy, clever, independent, stubborn, persistent, impulsive, intense.
2. **Providing enough exercise and mental stimulation.** Most Border Terriers are active go-getters. I recommend that you get your Border Terrier involved in regular obedience classes at the intermediate or advanced level, or in ongoing agility classes (an obstacle course for dogs). Or join an earth dog club, where terriers are encouraged to dig and tunnel after small critters (which are secured in a sturdy cage so they can't be harmed).
3. **Potential animal aggression.** Border Terriers are considerably less scrappy toward strange dogs than many other terrier breeds. But they are still a determined force to reckon with if they decide to initiate or accept a challenge to fight. Most terriers have strong instincts to chase and seize small fleeing creatures. This can make for conflict if you own a cat. It may be much worse than that if you own a pet rabbit or hamster!
4. **Yard security.** Terriers need a fenced yard in which to stretch their legs, but many terriers are clever escape artists who will go over or under fences in search of adventure. You may need higher fences than you might imagine for their small size. You may also need to sink wire into the ground along the fence line to thwart digging.
5. **Running away from you.** Like all dogs, Border Terriers must be taught to come when called. But I would only count on this breed obeying his training in an enclosed area. Terriers should not be trusted off-leash. The risk is too great that they will take off after anything that runs, oblivious to your frantic shouts.
6. **Potential barking.** Terriers are often too quick to sound the alarm at every new sight and sound. You have to be equally quick to stop them.
7. **Mind of his own.** Border Terriers are more willing to work with you than many other terriers. Many excel at the highest levels of obedience and agility competition. But the toughness that makes them suited to killing vermin can frustrate you when they decide to be stubborn. You must show them, through absolute consistency, that you mean what you say.

   To teach your Border Terrier to listen to you, "Respect Training" is mandatory. My [Border Terrier Training Page](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/training/) discusses the program you need.
8. **Grooming.** Border Terriers require clipping and trimming every few months. Breed purists may say that terrier coats should never be clipped because it makes the coat softer and more prone to matting. Instead they advocate *hand-stripping* (each dead hair pulled out so a new one can grow in its place). But in my opinion, stripping is too time-consuming and uncomfortable for the dog. Many groomers won't do it anymore. For pet dogs, I think clipping is just fine.
9. **Potential defensive reactions.** If you need to physically chastise a terrier, and you go beyond what THEY believe is a fair correction, terriers (as a group) are more likely than other breeds to growl or snap. As an obedience instructor, I have never had this happen with a *Border* Terrier, but I'm always extra careful when putting my hands on any terrier for a correction.

   I do not recommend terriers for homes with very young children. Many terriers will not tolerate any nonsense from little life forms whom they consider to be below themselves in importance. Many terriers are quick to react to teasing, and even to the normal clumsiness that comes with small children (accidental squeezing of their ears or pulling of whiskers or stepping on their paw). Many terriers are possessive of their food and toys and will defend these from all comers, including children.

## Are YOU the right owner for a Border Terrier?

Can you provide what this breed needs?

- Someone home most of the day
- Fenced yard (6-8 feet high, not an electronic/underground fence)
- No young children in the household
- No cats in the household
- Ample exercise after maturity – enough ongoing exercise that your Border Terrier stays slim and is tired enough to sleep contentedly and not get into mischief
- "Mental exercise" – interesting activities that keep the mind stimulated, such as a challenging dog sport (agility, rally obedience, earthdog, musical freestyle); challenging dog toys; a homemade obstacle course; tricks and games such as Musical Toys and Hide 'n Seek; instructions in my training book, [Teach Your Dog 100 English Words](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/books/TYD.html)
- Trimming/clipping – every few months
- An indoor lifestyle, except for exercise and bathroom breaks
- A meat-heavy diet, either [homemade](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/health/articles/feeding-homemade-dog-food.html) or [commercial](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/health/articles/feeding-healthy-natural-dog-food.html) – meat is expensive, so people with less money should opt for a small dog
- An owner with enough money to treat the [health problems](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/health/borderterriers.html) Border Terriers are prone to (pet health insurance can really help here!)
- Commitment to provide thorough socialization – introducing your Border Terrier to lots of people and other animals, diligently correcting any signs of misbehavior or aggression
- Commitment to establish the right Leader-Follower relationship with your Border Terrier, teaching him to [listen to you and do what you say](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/training/articles/dog-respect-training.html)

## Where can you buy or adopt a Border Terrier?

Border Terriers are middle-of-the-road in popularity in the United States. Out of 189 breeds in the American Kennel Club, where 1 is most popular and 189 is least popular, Border Terriers rank 86th. It's harder to find a Border Terrier compared to, say, a Westie or a Scottie. But you won't have too much trouble.

[Adopting From Dog Rescue Organizations](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/dog-breed-rescue.html)
Border Terriers are sometimes available from Dog Rescue groups. Border Terriers may be turned over to Rescue because of the dynamic traits that are common to all terrier types – scrappiness, bossiness, stubbornness, barking, chasing. You would need to provide thse dogs with the exercise and training that they are lacking.

[Adopting From Public Animal Shelters and Humane Societies](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/animal-shelters.html)
Border Terriers are only occasionally found here. Border Terrier Rescue groups keep their eyes peeled on shelters and humane societies across the country. On the off chance that a Border Terrier turns up at a shelter, the rescue group typically moves in quickly to take the dog.

[Buying From a Dog Breeder](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/dog-breeders.html)
You can buy a Border Terrier from a *show breeder*, who breeds Border Terriers to match a detailed standard of appearance for the dog show ring. Or you can buy from a *show/performance breeder* whose dogs also have high trainability for performance sports like rally obedience and dog agility.

You might also be able to buy a Border Terrier from people who "just breed pets" or "just had one litter." But should you? Be sure to read the article to learn more about these people.

Here's one difference between a responsible breeder and an irresponsible breeder – BOTH PARENTS of a Border Terrier puppy should have:

- a certificate from the Canine Eye Registry Foundation (CERF) – dated within the past year – certifying the dog to be free of **eye diseases**
- a certificate from the Orthopedic Foundation of America (OFA) or PennHip certifying the dog to have normal **hips**
- a certificate from the Orthopedic Foundation of America (OFA) certifying the dog to have normal **knees**
- a certificate from the Orthopedic Foundation of America (OFA) or a report from a veterinary cardiologist – dated within the past year – certifying that the dog has had an *Advanced Cardiac Exam* and has a normal **heart**

If a seller can't show you those certificates, the puppies are higher risk for health problems. You might choose to accept that risk. But then you need to be willing (and able) to pay a couple thousand bucks for future surgeries and lifelong meds if your Border Terrier ends up with cataracts, bad joints, and heart disease.

## Complete list of Border Terrier health problems

Orthopedic problems (luxating patella, hip dysplasia, elbow dysplasia) occur in Border Terriers, but only at low to moderate rates. The Orthopedic Foundation of America evaluated the hip X-rays of over 3100 Border Terriers and found 4% dysplastic. Of 500 elbow X-rays, 3% were dysplastic. The more serious Legg-Calve-Perthes disease has also been reported in Border Terriers.

Allergies (which cause itchy skin and can lead to pyoderma) are very common in all terrier breeds. Tumors (specifically, mast cell tumors) occur regularly in Border Terriers.

Eye diseases in Border Terriers include cataracts (appearing at 3-4 years old), lens luxation, and retinal dysplasia. Occasionally reported is progressive retinal atrophy (PRA) at 5-7 years old.

Serious diseases of increasing concern in Border Terriers are heart disease (septal defect and subaortic stenosis) and epilepsy.

A rare bone disease that can occur in Border Terriers is craniomandibular osteopathy.

### Preventing health problems

Some health problems are inherited. For example, if your dog inherits from his parents the genes for an eye disease called PRA, he will go blind and there's nothing anyone can do about it.

## Frequently asked questions

By [Michele Welton](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/about.html)

*Image hint — Border Terrier*

The Border is an unexaggerated, workman-like terrier. Where other terriers in the dog show ring are coiffed and fiery, up on their toes sparring with one another, the plain brown Border looks like he belongs in a Disney movie. In fact, he looks a bit like Benji... and this is just as his admirers want him to remain.

What kind of temperament and personality does the Border Terrier have?

The most descriptive words that come to my mind regarding Border Terrier temperament and behavior are:

spirited, athletic, tough, inquisitive, independent, determined hunter/chaser

I give you my honest opinions about Border Terrier temperament and personality traits and characteristics – positives AND negatives – in my dog breed review, [Border Terrier Temperament and Personality Traits](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/reviews/borderterriers.html) (What's Good About 'Em, What's Bad About 'Em).

What were Border Terriers used for?

This hardy hunter of fox, badger, and barnyard varmints originated in the Cheviot Hills on the border between England and Scotland.

What traits would help Border Terriers accomplish their work? That's right... keen senses, strong drives to chase and grab things that run, spunkiness, agility, toughness, determination, persistence, independent thinking, and loyalty to his owner.

How big are Border Terriers? Height and weight?

About 12-15 inches at the shoulder, and 12-18 pounds, which puts them in the [SMALL](../buying/articles/dog-breed-traits-size.html#smallsize) category.

What colors do Border Terriers come in?

Most Border Terriers are blue (dark blue/gray on a tan background) or grizzle (dark-tipped hairs on a red/tan background).

Do Border Terriers come in different "types"?

Sort of. In their native Britain, a few breeders specialize in producing Border Terriers for true working purposes, rather than for exhibition in the dog show ring.

But generally, you'll probably be acquiring a Border Terrier from a show breeder – preferably someone who ALSO demonstrates their dogs' responsiveness and trainability by participating with them in agility, obedience, etc.

How much [exercise](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/dog-breed-traits-exercise.html) do Border Terriers need?

A moderate to high amount. All terriers need brisk walks, plus a decent yard for romping and exploring. Jogging on soft ground would be appreciated! Play games of fetch and tug. Set up a miniature obstacle course and run around it with him, or get involved in an official canine sport called *agility.*

Most importantly, get your Border Terrier involved in an AKC terrier-focused activity called *Earthdog* – terriers love it. Another AKC activity that some terriers enjoy is called *Scentwork.*

You gotta do stuff with a Border Terrier! Otherwise they'll go over or under your fence in search of a more interesting place to live.

Do Border Terriers need a fenced yard?

Yes, with a HIGH solid fence that he can't climb! [Why a fenced yard?](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/do-dogs-need-fenced-yard.html) and [best and worst fences](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/dog-breed-traits-fencing.html).

Are Border Terriers [easy to train?](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/training/articles/dog-respect-training.html)

To be honest, no terrier breed is "easy" to train. Terriers are hardwired with an independent mind of their own. However, that doesn't mean you should just let them do what they want. It means you should expect more resistance than if you were teaching, say, an eager-to-please Poodle. But you should hold terriers just as accountable for bad behavior as you would any other breed.

The great news is that Border Terriers are more responsive than some other terriers, so if you have a confident personality, you should be able to establish a [healthy leader-follower relationship](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/training/articles/dog-respect-training.html) with this smart little dog. With the proper pecking order in place, combined with loads of exercise, a Border Terrier can be great fun to own.

Are Border Terriers easy to housebreak?

They're about average.

Are Border Terriers friendly with people?

Most Border Terriers are friendly with everyone, but some are more cautious with strangers.

Are Border Terriers [good with children?](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/dog-breed-traits-children.html)

I never recommend any terrier breed for children younger than about age 10. These breeds have a strong sense of pride and fairness and don't tend to put up with pushy children who step over the line. If you want a terrier, your kids should be older and have the kind of personality and behavior that can be relied upon to treat a dog with respect.

Are Border Terriers good [with other dogs?](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/dog-breed-traits-aggression-dogs.html)

Border Terriers are more sociable with other dogs than most other terriers. That said, if you're hoping to combine a terrier harmoniously with another dog in the household, I always recommend opposite sexes.

Are Border Terriers good [with cats?](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/dog-breed-traits-aggression-cats.html)

Well, with his heritage, you should expect that small running pets will be chased... and grabbed if possible. So a Border Terrier might be fine with the family cat sleeping on the sofa, but outside in the yard it might be a whole different story!

How much do Border Terriers [shed?](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/dog-breed-traits-shedding.html)

Border Terriers are average shedders, but more on the lighter side of average than the heavier side. Their broken, wiry coat sheds in the spring and fall.

Are Border Terriers [hypoallergenic?](../buying/articles/dog-breed-traits-shedding.html#allergic) Good for people with allergies?

If you're allergic to canine saliva, no. If you're allergic to dog dander... some people with (mild) dander allergies might not be badly affected by the Border Terrier. However, this breed isn't hypoallergenic to the same degree as a Poodle or Bichon Frise.

How much [grooming](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/dog-breed-traits-grooming.html) do Border Terriers need?

Hardly any brushing, but a couple of times a year, you'll need to trim/clip the coat short, else it will become shaggy and unkempt.

What behavior problems do Border Terriers tend to have?

As you might expect, leading the list is chasing things that run...  often followed by grabbing and killing things that run. Outside of a fenced yard, this breed should be kept on-leash.

Other Border Terrier behavioral issues include digging holes, digging under fences, climbing fences, and barking. Here's how to handle [behavior problems](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/training/articles/dog-behavior-problems.html).

What [health problems](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/health/borderterriers.html) might Border Terriers have?

Allergies that cause terribly itchy skin are very common in all terrier breeds. Eye diseases can lead to blindness. And heart disease and epilepsy are concerns in the Border Terrier.

What breeds are similar to the Border Terrier?

Like the Border, the [Jack Russell Terrier](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/reviews/jackrussellterriers.html), the [Smooth Fox Terrier](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/reviews/foxterriers.html) and the [Rat Terrier](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/reviews/ratterriers.html) are tough, high-energy terriers with an enthusiasm for stalking and chasing things. In addition, all four terriers are more responsive/trainable than most other terrier breeds.

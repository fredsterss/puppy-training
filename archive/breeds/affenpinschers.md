## Breed overview and temperament

Affenpinscher temperament, personality, training, behavior, pros and cons, advice, and information, by [Michele Welton](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/about.html)

*Image hint — Affenpinscher dog breed*

The "toy-terrier" Affenpinscher is spunkier than most other toy breeds. Somewhat high-strung, he tends to tremble when excited. If he perceives an invasion of his space by an approaching stranger or strange dog, he will try to appear intimidating by becoming raucous and blustery.

It might look comical, but if not nipped in the bud, an Affenpinscher may maintain his suspicious attitude even after a guest has been welcomed in. Socialization is very important with this breed to keep him under control.

The Affenpinscher adores his family and is fine with other family pets, especially when raised with them.

In the home, he is an inquisitive busybody who must check out new sights and sounds. His playful antics are delightfully entertaining as he bats toys around with his agile paws.

Affenpinschers do have a mind of their own, and without a firm hand can be obstinate and demanding, tossing tantrums or sulking when they don't get their own way.

It might make you laugh, but spoiling is not recommended for this breed, especially since he is so bright and does respond well to calm, patient training.

Like most terrier-type dogs, the Affenpinscher is proud and sensitive and does not take kindly to being teased. This breed is happiest in a home without young children.

If you want a dog who...

- Is small, easy to carry, and doesn't take up much space
- Looks like a wiry-coated terrier
- Is spunkier than most toys (his terrier background)
- Takes himself very seriously (which can be amusing to watch)
- Makes a keen watchdog
- Doesn't need a lot of exercise

An Affenpinscher may be right for you.

If you don't want to deal with...

- The fragility of toy breeds (see below)
- Suspiciousness toward strangers and strange dogs
- Stubbornness (a mind of his own)
- Regular brushing and trimming of the rough wiry coat
- Housebreaking difficulties
- Barking
- Waiting lists (hard to find)

An Affenpinscher may not be right for you.

- You can avoid some negative traits by choosing an ADULT dog from an [animal shelter](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/animal-shelters.html) or [rescue group](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/dog-breed-rescue.html). With an adult dog, you can easily see what you're getting, and plenty of adult Affenpinschers have already proven themselves not to have negative characteristics.
- If you want a puppy, you can avoid some negative traits by choosing the [right breeder](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/dog-breeders.html) and the [right puppy](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/how-to-choose-a-puppy.html).

Keep in mind that the inheritance of temperament  is less predictable than the inheritance of physical  traits such as size or shedding. Temperament and behavior are also shaped by raising and training.

More traits and characteristics of the Affenpinscher

If I was considering an Affenpinscher, I would be most concerned about...

1. **Fragility.** Too many people acquire a toy breed puppy without understanding how incredibly fragile a toy breed is. You can seriously injure or kill an Affenpinscher puppy by stepping on him or sitting on him. And Affenpinschers can seriously injure or kill themselves by leaping from your arms or off the back of your sofa. A larger dog can grab an Affenpinscher and break his neck with one quick shake. Owning a toy breed means constant supervision and surveillance of what's going on around your tiny dog. Affenpinschers must always be kept indoors, in a safely fenced yard, or on-leash. They are just too easy to injure when not under your complete control.
2. **Potential suspiciousness.** Many Affenpinschers will put on a display of excited ferociousness (in other words, they pitch a fit) when other people or animals approach them or anything they consider "theirs". It sounds funny, but isn't. Without [careful socialization](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/training/articles/dog-socializing.html), an Affenspinscher may be suspicious of everyone.
3. **Housebreaking.** Toy breeds are almost always difficult to housebreak. It is so easy for them to sneak behind a chair or under a small table, and it takes only a few seconds for the deed to be done. The results can be hard to see. When you don't see it, you don't correct it – and so the bad habit becomes established. If you hope to housebreak a toy breed, consistent crate training is mandatory. Toy breeds should not be loosed in the house for many months, until their small internal organs become strong enough for reliable control.
4. **Stubbornness and barking.** Affenpinschers have a mind of their own and will make you prove that you can make them do things. For example, they are often too quick to sound the alarm, so you must teach them not to bark excessively and to stop barkingwhen you tell them to. This only works if you establish the right relationship between you and your Affen, where you are the leader and he is the follower. You accomplish this through [Respect Training.](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/training/articles/dog-respect-training.html)
5. **Grooming.** To keep their wiry coat free of mats, Affenpinschers require regular brushing, and also clipping and trimming every few months.
6. **Potential health problems.** Most Affenpinschers live a good long life, but they are vulnerable to a devastating neurological disease called syringomyelia. They may also suffer from chronic allergies and itchy skin, as well as knee joint problems that can require expensive surgery. Read more about [Affenpinscher Health](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/health/affenpinschers.html).
7. **Finding one.** In the United States, only a few hundred Affenpinscher puppies are registered each year. This means you will probably need to get on a waiting list. Also their rarity means a small gene pool, which means inbreeding levels can be high. A higher inbreeding level increases the risk of many health problems.

## Are YOU the right owner for an Affenpinscher?

Can you provide what this breed needs?

- Someone home most of the day
- Fenced yard (not an electronic/underground fence)
- No young children in the household
- No large dogs in the household
- Extra safety precautions – ongoing supervision and surveillance of what's going on around your Affenpinscher, being careful where you step or sit, not allowing leaps from high furniture, keeping small objects off the floor so the dog can't choke, closing all gaps in fences and gates, etc.
- Enough ongoing exercise that your Affenpinscher stays slim and is tired enough to sleep contentedly and not get into mischief
- "Mental exercise" – interesting activities that keep the mind stimulated, such as challenging dog toys; a homemade obstacle course or dog agility classes; tricks and games such as Musical Toys and Hide 'n Seek; instructions in my training book, [Teach Your Dog 100 English Words](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/books/TYD.html)
- Brushing – moderate
- Trimming/clipping – every few months
- An indoor lifestyle, except for exercise and bathroom breaks
- A meat-heavy diet, either [homemade](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/health/articles/feeding-homemade-dog-food.html) or [commercial](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/health/articles/feeding-healthy-natural-dog-food.html) – meat is expensive, so people with less money should opt for a small dog
- An owner with enough money to treat the [health problems](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/health/affenpinschers.html) Affenpinschers are prone to (pet health insurance can really help here!)
- An owner who is okay with housebreaking taking a long time
- Commitment to provide thorough socialization – introducing your Affenpinscher to lots of people and other animals, diligently correcting any signs of misbehavior
- Commitment to establish the right Leader-Follower relationship with your Affenpinscher, teaching him to [listen to you and do what you say](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/training/articles/dog-respect-training.html)

## Where can you buy or adopt an Affenpinscher?

Affenpinschers are uncommon in the United States. Out of 189 breeds in the American Kennel Club, where 1 is most popular and 189 is least popular, Affenpinschers rank 149th. Maybe it's because to a lot of people, Affenpinschers look rather plain, even scruffy. I find that look appealing, but others may not.

[Dog Rescue Organizations](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/dog-breed-rescue.html)
You might find an Affenpinscher available from a Dog Rescue group. But not many Affenpinschers are turned over to Rescue groups, because not many people own them in the first place. Also Affenpinschers are not that difficult to live with, so fewer owners tend to give up on them and dump them into Rescue.

[Public Animal Shelters and Humane Societies](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/animal-shelters.html)
Affenpinschers are rarely found here for the same reasons listed above. Plus, Affenpinscher Rescue groups keep their eyes peeled on shelters and humane societies across the country. On the off chance that an Affenpinscher turns up at a shelter, the rescue group typically moves in quickly to take the dog.

[Dog Breeders](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/dog-breeders.html)
Affenpinscher breeders can be hard to find. You can buy an Affenpinscher from a *show breeder*, who breeds Affenpinschers to match a detailed standard of appearance for the dog show ring. You might also be able to buy an Affenpinscher from people who "just breed pets" or "just had one litter." But should you? Be sure to read the article to learn more about these people.

Here's one difference between a responsible breeder and an irresponsible breeder – BOTH PARENTS of an Affenpinscher puppy should have:

- a certificate from the Canine Eye Registry Foundation (CERF) – dated within the past year – certifying the dog to be free of **eye diseases**
- a certificate from the Orthopedic Foundation of America (OFA) certifying the dog to have normal **knees**

If a seller can't show you those certificates, the puppies are higher risk for health problems. You might choose to accept that risk. But then you need to be willing (and able) to pay a couple thousand bucks for future surgeries and lifelong meds if your Affenpinscher ends up with cataracts and bad knee joints.

## Complete list of Affenpinscher health problems

Orthopedic diseases include luxating patella (most common), Legg-Calve-Perthes disease (less common but more serious), and hip dysplasia (an appallingly high 18% rate, according to the Orthopedic Foundation of America).

Heart disease (patent ductus arteriosus and mitral valve disease) is a concern in Affenpinschers.

Cataracts can severely affect vision in this breed. Other eye diseases in Affenpinschers are glaucoma, corneal ulcers, dry eye, and tear duct disorders.

Hormonal/endocrine system diseases include hypothyroidism and Cushings disease.

Skin problems include allergies that cause itchy skin, pyoderma, and non-tumorous growths (sebaceous cysts).

Other health issues reported in Affenpinschers are blood-clotting disease (von Willebrand's), liver shunt, and hernias.

Affenpinschers are prone to dental disease and harmless episodes of reverse sneezing.

### Preventing health problems

Some health problems are inherited. For example, if your dog inherits from his parents the genes for an eye disease called PRA, he will go blind and there's nothing anyone can do about it.

## Frequently asked questions

By [Michele Welton](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/about.html)

*Image hint — Affenpinscher*

With only about two hundred puppies registered each year, difficult births, and tiny litters, well-bred Affenpinschers can be difficult to find. Expect a waiting list.

What kind of temperament and personality does the Affenpinscher have?

The most descriptive words that come to my mind regarding Affenpinscher temperament and behavior are: spunky, alert, inquisitive, loyal, full of character... and obstinate.

I give you my honest opinions about Affenpinscher temperament and personality traits and characteristics – positives AND negatives – in my dog breed review, [Affenpinscher Temperament and Personality Traits](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/reviews/affenpinschers.html) (What's Good About 'Em, What's Bad About 'Em).

How do you pronounce Affenpinscher?

AFF-en-pin-sher. *Affen* is German for **monkey** (because of the breed's bewhiskered face and large eyes). *Pinscher* is German for a terrier/biter type of dog. Affens are also related to schnauzers.

What were Affenpinschers used for?

Affenpinschers were watchdogs who also rid barns and other outbuildings of mice.

What traits would help Affenpinschers accomplish their work? That's right... keen senses, quick to bark, territorial instincts, spunkiness, strong chasing instincts. Just so you know!

How big are Affenpinschers? Height and weight?

About 9-12 inches at the shoulder, and 7-9 pounds, which puts them in the [TINY](../buying/articles/dog-breed-traits-size.html#tinysize) category. However, some Affens are so sturdy they seem more like SMALL dogs than TINY.

What colors do Affenpinschers come in?

Most Affenpinschers are black. In some countries, they *must* be black, while in North America they can also be black and silver, black and tan, red, and *belge* (a mixture of red/black/white).

How much [exercise](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/dog-breed-traits-exercise.html) do Affenpinschers need?

Just a few walks, plus games of fetch if you can encourage your Affen to play. Many of them will play for at least a little bit before losing interest. Set up a miniature obstacle course and run around it with him, coaxing, encouraging, and rewarding.

Do Affenpinschers need a fenced yard?

Yes. [Why?](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/do-dogs-need-fenced-yard.html) and [best and worst fences](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/dog-breed-traits-fencing.html).

Are Affenpinschers [easy to train?](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/training/articles/dog-respect-training.html)

Not particularly, no. With their terrier heritage, Affenpinschers do have a mind of their own. However, compared to true terriers, Affens are more willing to work with you. But you must be a firm owner who can establish a [healthy leader-follower relationship](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/training/articles/dog-respect-training.html) with this clever little dog.

Are Affenpinschers easy to housebreak?

Again, not really. All toy breeds are harder to housebreak for two reasons: (1) their internal organs are miniaturized and slow to develop; and (2) owners let them loose in the house far too early, they start "going" behind the sofa, and the bad habit is established. Sadly, the vast majority of non-housebroken dogs [are made that way by their owner](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/training/articles/dog-housebreaking.html).

Are Affenpinschers friendly with people?

With their beloved family, sure! These loyal dogs love their family, though they often prefer to be in the same room with you, but not necessarily on your lap.

With strangers, Affenpinschers are usually standoffish, and may even be suspicious, which can lead to excessive barking and even threatening behavior. You need to stop this the moment it starts.

Are Affenpinschers [good with children?](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/dog-breed-traits-children.html)

I don't recommend Affenpinschers for children younger than about age 10. This proud little breed can be a bit high-strung and defensive around very young children.

Are Affenpinschers good [with other dogs?](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/dog-breed-traits-aggression-dogs.html)

Affenpinschers tend to prefer their own breed, but they're usually fine with other dogs in their own family. Affenpinschers often dislike dogs they don't know, and may pitch a fit when they see one. This kind of nonsense needs to be stopped immediately.

Are Affenpinschers good [with cats?](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/dog-breed-traits-aggression-cats.html)

They're usually fine with cats in their own family. But they have excitable chasing instincts, so don't keep one with pet rodents or rabbits. Some Affens will even stalk birds.

How much do Affenpinschers [shed?](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/dog-breed-traits-shedding.html)

Affenpinschers are average shedders. Their coarse coat sheds moderately in the spring and fall, and occasionally a tiny bit of hair the rest of the year.

Are Affenpinschers [hypoallergenic?](../buying/articles/dog-breed-traits-shedding.html#allergic) Good for people with allergies?

If you're allergic to dog dander, no. If you're allergic to canine saliva, no. Affenspinschers are not hypoallergenic dogs.

How much [grooming](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/dog-breed-traits-grooming.html) do Affenpinschers need?

Just moderate brushing once a week, making sure to get out any mats under their "arms" and keeping the hair around their private parts trimmed or clipped short for sanitary toileting.

What behavior problems do Affenpinschers tend to have?

Most commonly I see excitability, darting around on the leash, and excessive barking. Also pugnaciousness toward strange dogs, and sometimes suspiciousness toward strangers. Here's [how to handle behavior problems](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/training/articles/dog-behavior-problems.html).

What [health problems](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/health/affenpinschers.html) might Affenpinschers have?

Loose "knee" joints (called luxating patella) on their hind legs is the most common serious problem that can occur. Also eye diseases such as cataracts, dental disease, and itchy skin/allergies.

What breeds are similar to an Affenpinscher?

I might say the [Brussels Griffon](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/reviews/brusselsgriffons.html), which was actually created from a blending of Affenpinschers, Pugs, and English Toy Spaniels.

With those pug and spaniel genes, the Brussels Griffon has a flatter face than the Affen, and thus more health issues, especially respiratory issues. I also find the Brussels Griffon to be a bit harder to train than the Affenpinscher.

Where can I buy an Affenpinscher puppy?

Affenpinschers are uncommon in the United States, perhaps because they look rather plain, even scruffy. I find that look appealing, but many people aren't impressed.

Because the breed is uncommon, reputable Affenpinscher breeders can be hard to find. Expect a waiting list. [Read more.](../buying/affenpinschers.html#where)

Where can I adopt an older Affenpinscher?

Not many Affenpinschers are turned over to [Rescue](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/dog-breed-rescue.html) groups, because not many people own them in the first place. Also Affens are not that difficult to live with, so fewer owners tend to give up on them.

For the same reasons, Affenpinschers are rarely found in [Animal Shelters or Humane Societies](https://www.yourpurebredpuppy.com/buying/articles/animal-shelters.html).
